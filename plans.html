Before you choose

Questions worth asking

The things people ask us most before picking a plan — answered plainly.

Third-party cover is the legal minimum: it pays for damage or injury you cause to someone else, but nothing for your own vehicle. Comprehensive adds own-damage protection — accidents, fire, theft and natural calamities — plus personal accident cover for you. If your vehicle is newer or financed, comprehensive is almost always the right call.

Four things drive it: your vehicle’s insured declared value (IDV), its age, where it is registered and driven, and the add-ons you pick. Your no-claim bonus then discounts the own-damage portion by up to 50%. The third-party component is fixed by regulation and does not vary between insurers.

Yes. Your no-claim bonus belongs to you, not to your previous insurer. Share your expiring policy number and last renewal notice when you switch, and we will apply the same NCB slab — up to 50% — to your own-damage premium. It stays intact as long as you renew within 90 days of expiry.

Add-ons such as zero depreciation, engine protection and roadside assistance can be added mid-term; the cost is charged pro-rata for the remaining policy period. Removing an add-on takes effect at your next renewal, since cover already in force cannot be withdrawn retrospectively.
